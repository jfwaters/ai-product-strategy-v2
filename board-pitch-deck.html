The Case · 1 of 3

Why now

Federal pre-disaster mitigation funding was cancelled in 2025, restored by a court and relaunched in 2026 with new priorities.
Every community's funding plan is being rebuilt against new rules, while the tools in use still stop at mapping risk or checking one project's benefit-cost ratio. Nothing turns risk, costs and funding rules into a portfolio a council will approve. The same turmoil cuts both ways: unstable federal funding means unstable buyer budgets, which is why the ask is staged.

The Risks

What could break this

Trust / Failure
The front-page story is a council that applies for federal money based on our recommendation, only for FEMA to reject the project as ineligible, or a buyout in a low-income neighborhood that gets deprioritized without anyone noticing. Our safeguards:
  • Every number comes from the optimizer, and 100% of AI explanations are checked against it.
  • Eligibility statements must cite a funding rule, or they're blocked.
  • Anything below 70% confidence, or any change that affects households, goes to a named human reviewer before export.
  • The weakness: our prototype currently breaks one of its own funding rules. That's the first fix, measured by a test set growing from 10 to 25 cases.
Scale / Governance
Costs and model quality aren't what break at 10x. What breaks is funding-rule upkeep and the human review queue, which grow with every new state, not every new user. Today's cost model budgets a quarter of one person for rule upkeep, and that's too little. The ask funds a full-time grants-policy expert, and we'll plan growth around "states supported" rather than seats. Feeding customer corrections back into the product depends on contract clauses public agencies may refuse; we'll know from the first pilots.
Competitive
The scenario that forces a kill is Esri bundling a "good enough" portfolio and funding module into ArcGIS, sold to the state offices that already use it, before we have 3 signed pilots. If that happens, we stop competing on the core product and become the funding-rules layer that plugs into ArcGIS. The closest competitor, Jupiter Intelligence with Arcadis, already models adaptation returns but has shown no funding-rules logic. That's the gap we have to close first.
The Roadmap

How this moves through time

Horizon 1
Now · 0–3 months
  • Pilot interviews with 10 consulting firms and state mitigation offices
  • Run the golden dataset against the prototype and fix funding-rule errors (e.g., fire station funded from the clean-water loan)
  • Require a reason for every planner override, recorded in the audit trail
  • Kill-switch basics: AI gateway, backup provider and explanation evals
  • Data-sharing and correction-sharing clauses in pilot contracts
  • Test hybrid pricing ($150 base + $300 per package) and prepaid bundles with pilot buyers
Horizon 2
Next · 3–9 months
  • Production optimizer and funding-rules engine (BRIC, FMA, HMGP plus 2 states), with rule citations on every eligibility statement
  • Confidence tiers and a "show the math" view
  • Grant drafting add-on, priced per package
  • Cascading model routing and prompt caching
  • Funding-rule change digest ("what changed for your plan")
  • ArcGIS export for council maps (partner, not build)
  • Governance operations: weekly evals, monthly review, override triage
Horizon 3
Bet · 9–18 months
  • Anonymized cost benchmark across customers, starting with state offices
  • Outcome calibration loop (estimated vs. bid vs. final costs) feeding confidence tiers
  • Second hazard type (wildfire or extreme heat)
  • Outcome-based pricing option (share of funding won), pending a 2 CFR 200 review
